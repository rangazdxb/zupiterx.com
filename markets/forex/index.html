What forex is

Currencies, traded in pairs.

The foreign-exchange market is where currencies change hands — a decentralised network of banks, funds, companies and individual traders rather than a single exchange. It exists because the global economy does: trade, investment and travel all create demand to swap one currency for another.

For traders, FX offers deep, continuous two-way markets in the world’s major currencies. Prices respond to interest rates, economic data and sentiment throughout each global trading session.

How it works

Four things to understand first.

01

Quotes come in pairs

Every FX price expresses one currency in terms of another. EUR/USD at 1.0824 means one euro buys 1.0824 US dollars. You always trade the base currency against the quote currency.

02

Pips measure movement

For most pairs a pip is the fourth decimal place. Position size determines what each pip is worth in money — the first calculation every new trader should learn.

03

Sessions shape the day

Liquidity moves with the sun: Asia-Pacific opens the week, Europe takes over through the London session, and the US session closes the day. Activity is typically highest where sessions overlap.

04

The spread is a cost

The difference between bid and ask is part of what a trade costs. Spreads vary by pair and by market conditions, and typically widen around major news events.

Trading hours

FX trades continuously from Monday morning Asia-Pacific to Friday evening New York, with brief daily maintenance windows possible. Exact tradable hours per pair are defined in the trading platform.

Market drivers

What moves currencies.

  • Interest-rate expectations reshape demand across major currency pairs.
  • Economic releases can quickly change momentum and session activity.
  • Liquidity often increases where London and New York hours overlap.
  • Political and central-bank developments influence longer market cycles.
